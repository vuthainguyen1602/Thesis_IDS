\section{I.\ PROBLEM AND CONTRIBUTIONS}
% =====================================================================

\begin{frame}{Edge intrusion detection: three open gaps}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}
      Detectors now \emph{fit} on single-board hardware --- but a monolithic
      one classifies \key{every} flow and saturates an 8\,GB ARM board.
      \vspace{0.6em}

      \textbf{What the literature does not tell us}
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item Everything is measured on a \bad{single device}.
        \item Throughput, latency and \bad{energy} are never reported together.
        \item Offline scores come from \bad{leaky protocols}.
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.42\textwidth}
      \begin{alertblock}{Our question}
        How should inference be \emph{placed} across a constrained two-node
        edge cluster?
      \end{alertblock}
      \begin{exampleblock}{Our answer}
        Split it: a cheap \key{autoencoder gate} on board \#1, the heavy
        \key{PySpark classifier} on board \#2, joined by a Kafka topic.
      \end{exampleblock}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \note{Three gaps: single-device evaluation, energy never reported next to
  latency, and offline numbers taken from leaky pipelines. Which model to
  deploy is the other half of the question. We attack all three with one
  system.}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 0:35
\begin{frame}{Contributions}
  The parts are standard (autoencoder, Kafka, Spark MLlib). \key{No new
  detector}: the contribution is what we measured on real edge hardware.
  \vspace{0.3em}
  \begin{enumerate}\small
    \item A \key{placement study} of a two-stage IDS on a two-board Jetson
          cluster: single node, pipeline split on one and on two boards,
          horizontal Kafka scaling, Spark standalone cluster.
    \item An \key{end-to-end evaluation}: sustained capacity, latency with
          queueing, energy per verdict, and \key{precision / recall / F1 after
          the gate} --- on CICIDS2017 and on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 replay.
    \item A \key{same-model engine baseline}: PySpark vs.\ NumPy vs.\ ONNX
          Runtime on the deployed forest.
    \item \key{Leakage-aware provenance}: the deployed model is the one
          selected by our offline Spark ML evaluation under a leakage-free
          protocol.
  \end{enumerate}
  \note{We do not claim a new detector or a new distributed method. The
  contribution is the measurement: which placement pays, why, and what the
  gate costs in detection. The model we deploy is the one that survived a
  strict offline audit.}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 0:40
\begin{frame}{Deployment modes and what we measure}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.58\textwidth}
      \begin{description}\footnotesize
        \item[Single node] full pipeline on one board, gate off or on.
        \item[A: Pipeline split] gate on \#1, classifier on \#2 (our design).
        \item[A1: Split, one board] gate and classifier as two processes on \#2.
        \item[B: Horizontal] both boards run the full role (gate off).
        \item[C: Spark cluster] host as master, both boards as workers.
      \end{description}
      \vspace{0.3em}
      \footnotesize \key{Every flow is followed from send to verdict}, queueing
      included. \key{Sustained capacity}: the highest rate at which every flow
      gets its verdict within 10\,s of the load.
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.40\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{Setup}
        \footnotesize
        2$\times$ Jetson Orin Nano Super\\ (6-core A78AE, 8\,GB, $\approx$67\,TOPS)\\[0.15em]
        PySpark 3.4.1, Kafka 3.5, Wi-Fi LAN\\[0.15em]
        10{,}000 test-split flows, 15\% attacks\\
        $+$ 10{,}000 CSE-CIC-IDS2018 flows\\[0.15em]
        Rate sweep 1--40 flows/s, 2 runs each\\[0.15em]
        Energy via \texttt{tegrastats}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \note{Latency is measured from the moment a flow is sent to the moment its
  verdict is written, so time spent waiting in a queue counts. A fresh Kafka
  consumer group per run, so no run inherits another's backlog.}
\end{frame}

\section{III.\ RESULTS AND CONCLUSION}
% =====================================================================

\begin{frame}{Result 1: decoupling the stages sustains 7--9$\times$ the load}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.58\textwidth}
      \centering\small
      \fitwidth{%
      \begin{tabular}{lccc}
        \toprule
        Mode & Flows/s & e2e p95 (s) & J / verdict \\
        \midrule
        Single node         & 2.5            & 11.3          & 3.04 \\
        Single node + gate  & $\approx$2.5   & 7.8           & 3.04 \\
        B: Horizontal       & 3.3            & 8.2           & 3.20 \\
        C: Spark cluster    & 9--17          & 5.2           & 1.15 \\
        A1: Split, one board & \num{17.8}    & 5.3           & \num{0.32} \\
        \textbf{A: Split}   & \num{22.4}     & \num{7.1}     & \num{0.60} \\
        \bottomrule
      \end{tabular}
      }
      \\[0.3em]
      \raggedright\scriptsize
      \textbf{Flows/s}: sustained, every flow done within 10\,s.
      \textbf{e2e p95}: send to verdict, queueing included.
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.39\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}\small
        \setlength{\itemsep}{0.4em}
        \item \bad{The gate inside the classifier's process gains nothing.}
        \item \good{As a separate process on the same board: 7$\times$}, at
              0.32\,J per verdict.
        \item A second board adds a quarter (22.4) at twice the energy.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \takeaway{\textbf{Decoupling, not the second board, pays}: one board
  running the split is the efficient choice, and a second board buys headroom.}
  \note{Single node and horizontal run with the gate off, as in the submitted
  version. Energy is measured at each mode's sustained rate: 2.0, 2.0, 3.9,
  9.5, 17.8 and 18.4 flows per second. Tail latency is seconds in every mode because one PySpark batch takes seconds. A1 was measured in a later session
  with the same boards, models and replay file. At 30 flows per second one
  repeat drained in 16 seconds. Gate skip is about 89 percent in every gated
  mode. Median latency in split mode is about one second. Single node plus gate: the 10 second rule puts it at 1.9 only because one repeat drained in 10.6 seconds; at 3 flows per second it delivers 2.4 to 2.5 like the single node. Spark cluster: at 20 flows per second one repeat kept up at 17.1 and the other did not, so its capacity is only known to lie between about 9 and 17.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Result 3: what the gate costs, end to end}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}
      \centering\small
      \fitwidth{%
      \begin{tabular}{lcccc}
        \toprule
        Mode & Attacks & Missed: gate / clf & FP & F1 \\
        \midrule
        \textbf{A: full replay} & 1{,}518 & 463 / 0 & 0 & \num{0.820} \\
        A1: split, one board    & 882     & 278 / 0 & 0 & 0.813 \\
        C: Spark cluster        & 381     & 115 / 0 & 0 & 0.822 \\
        Gate off                & 242     & -- / 0  & 0 & 1.000 \\
        \bottomrule
      \end{tabular}
      }
      \\[0.3em]
      \raggedright\scriptsize Final verdicts of the deployed pipeline against
      the replay labels. Full replay: all 10{,}000 flows once, 20 flows/s for
      500\,s. Classifier alone, offline: F1 0.996.
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.42\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}\small
        \setlength{\itemsep}{0.4em}
        \item \good{Precision 100\%}: no false alarm on 8{,}483 benign flows.
        \item \bad{Recall 69.5\%}: every missed attack was \emph{skipped by the
              gate}. The classifier made no error on what it received.
        \item The gate's threshold alone sets the end-to-end F1.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \takeaway{The capacity gain is paid for in recall: the gate is a
  \key{tunable trade-off} (operating curve in backup), not a free filter.}
  \note{The reviewers asked for exactly this: precision, recall and F1 of the
  whole deployed pipeline, not the offline classifier. The deployed threshold
  is the 0.995 quantile of benign validation errors, fixed before any test.
  The capacity-window rows overlap because every window replays the sample
  from its first row. The full replay covers every flow once.}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 0:50
\begin{frame}{Result 4: another network --- CSE-CIC-IDS2018}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}
      \centering\small
      \fitwidth{%
      \begin{tabular}{lcc}
        \toprule
        Replayed flows & CICIDS2017 & CSE-CIC-IDS2018 \\
        \midrule
        Gate forwards           & 11\%   & \bad{30\%} \\
        Benign it forwards      & 0.6\%  & 28\% \\
        Sustained (flows/s)     & 22.4   & \bad{$\approx$8} \\
        Recall / F1             & 69.5\% / 0.82 & \bad{0.8\% / 0.016} \\
        Classifier alone, recall & 99.8\% & 1.8\% \\
        \bottomrule
      \end{tabular}
      }
      \\[0.3em]
      \raggedright\scriptsize Same deployed split, models trained on
      CICIDS2017, 10{,}000 flows each, replayed once. Precision 100\% in both.
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.42\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}\small
        \setlength{\itemsep}{0.4em}
        \item The gate flags new benign traffic as anomalous: the classifier
              gets \bad{3$\times$ the load}.
        \item The classifier itself misses almost every 2018 attack.
        \item Offline: 1\% labelled target flows restore F1 to 0.92.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \takeaway{Neither detection \emph{nor capacity} transfers: a new network
  needs a \key{recalibrated gate} and a \key{retrained classifier}.}
  \note{Reviewer one asked for a second dataset, so we replayed CSE-CIC-IDS2018
  through the deployed boards without retraining. Capacity is not a property
  of the pipeline alone: the gate's skip ratio depends on the traffic, and
  with it the load on the classifier. At 12 flows per second a repeat drained
  in 29 seconds. The offline cross-dataset study, 18 runs, gives AUC-PR 0.48
  from 2017 to 2018 and 0.19 the other way.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Result 5: the price of one unified stack}
  We serve with Spark for \emph{consistency with distributed training}.
  What does that cost? The \emph{deployed forest}, exported, same board:
  \vspace{0.5em}
  \centering
  \small
  \fitwidth{%
  \begin{tabular}{lccc}
    \toprule
    Engine & Flows/s & Batch p95 (ms) & mJ per flow \\
    \midrule
    PySpark (deployed) & 1.4      & 7515  & 1506 \\
    NumPy              & 54.8     & 194   & 11.3 \\
    \textbf{ONNX Runtime} & \num{10{,}092} & \num{1.2} & \num{0.087} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  }
  \\[0.2em]{\scriptsize Batch 10, as deployed, with identical predictions on every
  flow. mJ per flow: energy above the board's idle power. A Spark call costs
  $\approx$7\,s at any batch up to 1{,}000, so at batch 10{,}000 the gap is
  $\approx$27$\times$ (backup).}
  \takeaway{ONNX Runtime serves the same forest \num{$\approx$7{,}200$\times$}
  faster. Target for the next iteration: \key{train on Spark, serve with
  ONNX/TensorRT}.}
  \note{The JVM costs us more than three orders of magnitude, and that sets
  the roadmap. Batch size 10. If asked whether bigger batches fix it: a Spark
  call costs about seven seconds whatever the batch, so the gap shrinks to
  about 27 times at ten thousand flows, but a streaming IDS at 20 flows per
  second would wait eight minutes to fill such a batch.}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 0:40
\begin{frame}{Conclusion}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.53\textwidth}
      \textbf{What we showed}
      \begin{itemize}\small
        \item Reproducible edge IDS: Kafka $+$ PySpark on Jetson Orin Nano Super.
        \item Decoupled stages: \good{2.5 $\rightarrow$ 17.8 flows/s} on one
              board (0.32\,J), \good{22.4} on two (0.60\,J), e2e p95
              $\approx$7\,s.
        \item End to end: \good{precision 100\%}, \bad{recall 69.5\%}, F1 0.82.
              Every miss is a gate skip.
        \item ONNX serves the same forest $\approx$7{,}200$\times$ faster.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.44\textwidth}
      \textbf{Limitations}
      \begin{itemize}\footnotesize
        \item Two-node lab cluster over Wi-Fi; replayed traffic.
        \item No transfer to CSE-CIC-IDS2018 without retraining.
        \item Kafka and the database stay on a host PC.
        \item GPU unused (CPU-only pipeline); evasion untested.
      \end{itemize}
      \textbf{Next}
      \begin{itemize}\footnotesize
        \item ONNX/TensorRT serving; live traffic; retraining under drift.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vfill
  \centering
  \key{Thank you --- questions?}
  \note{Recap: decoupling gives seven to nine times the load at a fraction of
  the energy. The price is about thirty percent of attacks skipped by the
  gate. On another dataset both detection and capacity fall, so a new
  network needs recalibration and retraining.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Backup: dependence on the host PC}
  \begin{itemize}\footnotesize
    \item Kafka, PostgreSQL, InfluxDB and Grafana run on the host: flows enter
          through its broker, verdicts are stored there. \bad{Host down
          $\Rightarrow$ detection stops}, although both boards are healthy.
    \item With PySpark the host is \emph{not} the bottleneck: the boards saturate
          first, and a host-to-board round trip takes 4--9\,ms on Wi-Fi.
    \item It still bounds scaling: each added board is one more consumer of the
          same broker and one more writer to the same database (one insert per
          verdict).
    \item Field deployment: broker on or next to the board that receives the
          flow export, verdicts buffered locally and forwarded in batches,
          Kafka replicated across the boards. \key{Not measured.}
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Backup: measurement protocol}
  \begin{itemize}\small
    \item Host-side orchestration starts pipeline roles on the Jetsons over
          SSH, drives the load, collects every verdict.
    \item Each verdict stores the flow's send time and replay row: latency is
          send to verdict, \emph{queueing included}. Skip ratio and attack
          recall come from the flows themselves.
    \item Rate sweep per mode. A rate is sustained when every flow sent in the
          45\,s window gets its verdict within 10\,s after it. 2 runs per rate.
    \item Fresh Kafka consumer group per run, so no run inherits a backlog.
    \item Tiers batch 20 flows or flush after 1\,s.
    \item Energy: \texttt{tegrastats}, 30\,s idle baseline then load at the
          sustained rate. Two-node modes sum both boards.
  \end{itemize}

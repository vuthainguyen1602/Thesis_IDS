\note{Good morning. I will show what a second edge board actually buys you
when you split an ML intrusion detector across two Jetson Orin Nano Super
devices. Seven minutes, three results.}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 0:50
\begin{frame}{Edge intrusion detection: three open gaps}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}
      Detectors now \emph{fit} on single-board hardware --- but a monolithic
      one classifies \key{every} flow and saturates an 8\,GB ARM board.
      \vspace{0.4em}

      \textbf{What the literature does not tell us}
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item Everything is measured on a \bad{single device}. How should
              detection be \emph{placed} across boards?
        \item Throughput, latency and \bad{energy} are never reported together.
        \item Offline scores come from \bad{leaky protocols}.
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.42\textwidth}
      \begin{alertblock}{Our question}
        Which model to deploy --- and \emph{how} to distribute its inference
        across a constrained two-node edge cluster?
      \end{alertblock}
      \begin{exampleblock}{Our answer}
        Split the detector: a cheap \key{autoencoder gate} on board \#1, the
        heavy \key{PySpark classifier} on board \#2, joined by a Kafka topic.
      \end{exampleblock}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \note{Three gaps: single-device evaluation, energy never reported next to
  latency, and offline numbers taken from leaky pipelines. We attack all
  three with one system.}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 0:35
\begin{frame}{Contributions}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item A \key{two-stage distributed IDS}: anomaly gate $+$ Spark classifier,
          physically separated across two boards.
    \item \key{Three deployment modes} for a two-node Jetson cluster ---
          pipeline split, horizontal Kafka scaling, Spark standalone cluster.
    \item An \key{end-to-end evaluation}: throughput, latency percentiles,
          per-node CPU/RAM \emph{and} energy per verdict.
    \item \key{Leakage-aware provenance} --- the deployed model is exactly the
          one our companion study selected under a leakage-free protocol.
          Open code and configurations.
  \end{enumerate}
  \vfill
  \centering\footnotesize
  \texttt{github.com/vuthainguyen1602/Thesis\_IDS} (branch \texttt{develop})
  \note{Four contributions. The fourth matters: the model we deploy is not a
  fresh model, it is the one that survived a strict offline audit.}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 0:40
\begin{frame}{Three deployment modes, one workload}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.58\textwidth}
      \begin{description}
        \item[Single node] full pipeline in one process --- the reference.
        \item[A: Pipeline split] gate on \#1, classifier on \#2 (our design).
        \item[B: Horizontal] both boards run the \emph{full} role, sharing a
              Kafka consumer group.
        \item[C: Spark cluster] host as master, both boards as workers.
      \end{description}
      \vspace{0.4em}
      \footnotesize Throughput is counted as \key{final verdicts per second}
      --- a flow is decided at the gate \emph{or} at the classifier --- so
      forwarded flows are never double-counted.
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.40\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{Setup}
        \footnotesize
        2$\times$ Jetson Orin Nano Super\\ (6-core A78AE, 8\,GB, $\approx$67 TOPS)\\[0.3em]
        PySpark 3.4.1, Kafka 3.5, Wi-Fi LAN\\[0.3em]
        CICIDS2017 replay at 100 flows/s\\[0.3em]
        $\geq$3 repetitions, warmup excluded\\[0.3em]
        Energy via \texttt{tegrastats}, idle subtracted
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \note{Four configurations, identical workload. The verdict-per-second
  definition is what makes the modes comparable.}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 1:20
\begin{frame}{Result 1: splitting the pipeline buys 24$\times$ throughput}
  \centering
  \small
  \fitwidth{%
  \begin{tabular}{lcccc}
    \toprule
    Mode & Throughput & p95 latency & Gate skip & Energy/verdict \\
         & (flows/s)  & (ms)        & (\%)      & (mJ) \\
    \midrule
    Single node        & 2.60 $\pm$ 0.41 & 12.3 $\pm$ 3.0  & (inline) & 2490 \\
    \textbf{A: Pipeline split} & \num{62.5 $\pm$ 0.6}  & \num{13.1 $\pm$ 2.6}  & \num{95.8} & \num{178} \\
    B: Horizontal      & 5.9 $\pm$ 1.4   & 21.6 $\pm$ 19.2 & (inline) & 2250 \\
    C: Spark cluster   & 90.0 $\pm$ 25.6 & 23.9 $\pm$ 17.8 & 97.9     & 119 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  }

  \par\vspace{0.2em}
  \raggedright\footnotesize
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}
        \item \good{A: $24\times$ throughput at unchanged p95} --- the gate
              absorbs 95.8\% of benign traffic.
        \item \bad{B stays Spark-bound} ($2\times$): every node still classifies.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}
        \item C is fastest on average but $\pm$26 flows/s and needs the host master.
        \item Energy amortised over $24$--$35\times$ more verdicts:
              \good{2.5 $\rightarrow$ 0.12--0.18\,J}.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \takeaway{\textbf{Pipeline split is the recommended default} --- the highest
  throughput \emph{per unit of variance}; cluster mode is for models that exceed
  single-node memory.}
  \note{This is the core table. Twenty-four times, at the same p95. Mode B
  proves the gain comes from role separation, not from adding a board. Energy
  is total-board per verdict: the active power delta was below the tegrastats
  noise floor at this load.}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 0:45
\begin{frame}{Result 3: the honest price of one unified stack}
  We serve with Spark for \emph{consistency with distributed training}.
  What does that cost? Same forest, same 29 deployed features, same board:
  \vspace{0.5em}
  \centering
  \small
  \fitwidth{%
  \begin{tabular}{lcccc}
    \toprule
    Engine & Throughput (flows/s) & p95 (ms) & RAM (\%) & Energy/inf.\ (mJ) \\
    \midrule
    PySpark (deployed) & 22.6   & 532  & 24.3 & 71.6 \\
    scikit-learn       & 204.4  & 49.8 & 13.3 & 2.91 \\
    \textbf{ONNX Runtime} & \num{7870.8} & \num{1.1} & 15.7 & \num{0.06} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  }
  \takeaway{ONNX Runtime sustains \num{$348\times$} the deployed PySpark
  throughput. We report this as a \emph{target for the next iteration} ---
  export to ONNX/TensorRT for serving, keep Spark for training.}
  \note{We could have hidden this. Instead: the JVM costs us three orders of
  magnitude, and that sets the roadmap.}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 0:40
\begin{frame}{Conclusion}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.53\textwidth}
      \textbf{What we showed}
      \begin{itemize}
        \item A reproducible distributed edge IDS: Kafka $+$ PySpark on a
              Jetson Orin Nano Super cluster.
        \item Splitting gate and classifier across boards:
              \good{2.6 $\rightarrow$ 62.5 flows/s} at p95 $\approx$13\,ms,
              gate skipping 95.8\%.
        \item Energy per verdict: \good{2.5\,J $\rightarrow$ 0.18\,J}.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.44\textwidth}
      \textbf{Limitations}
      \begin{itemize}\footnotesize
        \item Two-node lab cluster over Wi-Fi; CICIDS2017 replay, not live traffic.
        \item The $\approx$67-TOPS GPU is unused by the CPU-only pipeline.
        \item Evasive traffic untested.
      \end{itemize}
      \textbf{Next}
      \begin{itemize}\footnotesize
        \item TensorRT/ONNX in the streaming path.
        \item Live traffic; federated retraining under drift.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Backup: measurement protocol}
  \begin{itemize}\small
    \item Host-side orchestration starts pipeline roles on the Jetsons over
          SSH, drives the load, collects per-node logs.
    \item $\geq$3 repetitions per configuration; warmup traffic \emph{excluded}
          from the measured window (no JVM/JIT inflation of early batches).
    \item Metric queries aligned to the exact load window, not a trailing range.
    \item Latency percentiles per node over \emph{raw per-flow} samples;
          cluster p95 is conservatively the \emph{worst node's} p95.
    \item End-to-end send-to-verdict latency from producer timestamps under
          NTP-synchronised clocks (excludes DB-write cost).
    \item Energy: \texttt{tegrastats}, 30\,s idle baseline then load window;
          two-node modes sum both boards.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
